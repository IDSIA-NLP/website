Research

Languages, dialects and multilinguality

How language models deal with dialects, scripts and less-resourced languages, and how to make them fairer across languages.

Language models work best for a handful of large languages. We study why, starting from the text itself: how words are split into pieces (tokenization), how scripts and dialects are represented inside models, and how much information text carries in different languages.

The goal is language technology that serves speakers of all languages and dialects more equally. Our work on tokenization bias in dialects received a Senior Area Chair award at EMNLP 2025.

Projects

BM-TOK

Balancing multilingual token vocabularies

How to build the vocabulary of a multilingual language model so that less-resourced languages and non-Latin scripts are represented fairly.

Research 2026–2027

LPHI-mRAG

Language preference and hallucination in multilingual retrieval-augmented generation

Do language models prefer some languages over others when answering from documents? We test this for chatbots that answer questions from a collection of documents in several languages.

Research 2026

M2P2

Medical, multilingual and privacy-preserving NLP in the clinical domain

Makes modern language technology usable in Swiss hospitals: across the country's languages and without exposing patient data.

Research 2025–2026 · Swiss National Science Foundation (SNF)

ELDI

Dialect identification in short texts

Developed methods to recognise the dialect of short texts such as social media posts, focusing on varieties of Arabic.

Research 2023

INCdid

Dialect identification in noisy text

Developed methods to identify dialects in short, noisy texts, especially between closely related language varieties.

Conversational recommendation of performers
